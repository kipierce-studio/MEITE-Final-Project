GoNoodle:
Where Movement Meets Wellbeing

    SWOT Analysis

    Strengths

    GoNoodle is uniquely positioned across the United States. In a constantly expanding industry, GoNoodle has positioned itself in almost 600,000 classrooms across the US (How This EdTech Entrepreneur Got GoNoodle Into 600,000 Classrooms, n.d.). With such a strong stance in K-5 spaces, they make it very difficult for new entities to infiltrate. GoNoodle has centered itself on brand trust and a high-quality freemium product line, layering a paid offer on top (How This EdTech Entrepreneur Got GoNoodle Into 600,000 Classrooms, n.d.). This has set a precedent for other companies to compete with. With strong connections in the K-5 space, GoNoodle was easily able to funnel their newest addition SuperNoodle into already engaged schools, rather than fighting cold accounts. Another strength of GoNoodle is their top-notch track record with other brands. GoNoodle has strong, recognizable content that features their own unique characters, like Moose Fabio, as well as brands with Disney, Nickelodeon, and Dr. Seuss Enterprises who allow GoNoodle to use their characters (Covert, 2025). These connections show their users that they are a trusted source and backed by trusted companies. These partnerships allow GoNoodle to package content into licensable series and media specials, which they are able to monetize.

    Weaknesses

    Many of GoNoodle’s weaknesses center on the tension between free, broach-reach products and the need to build sustainable and predictable revenue. Currently, GoNoodle is a relatively small group, with only about 40-50 employees (GoNoodle Knowledge Base, n.d.). This small employee base creates a limited reach for the company as a whole. Another weakness of GoNoodle is where their funding is concentrated. They have much of their revenue in brand and media partnerships, leaving them vulnerable to revenue stopping. In the future, they may need to diversify their revenue streams. The start of SuperNoodle will help with this, but establishing the platform as reliable will take time. This connects to another weakness of GoNoodle. They are at risk of relying too heavily on free distribution. Now that they have established themselves by offering such an array of free content, they will likely struggle introducing fees and premiums with new platforms. To combat this, GoNoodle should keep their monetization focused on their B2B partnerships, with districts and schools alone, while leaving content for families and teacher experiences free (Gonoodle.Com/Company/p/Who-We-Are, n.d.).

    Opportunities

    There are a range of external trends that may prove to be beneficial for GoNoodle to build and expand. First, society is seeing a rising investment in Social-Emotional Learning in school programming. Districts are continuing to prioritize the industry, and SuperNoodle is well positioned to fill this need. To maximize their investment in this opportunity, GoNoodle could create bundles for districts and schools that promote both movement and Social-Emotional Learning and increase their own funding. Another opportunity that is unique to GoNoodle is the underdevelopment of the at-home and out-of-school market for Social-Emotional Learning. There is not a strong programming for parents and communities to use with their children and GoNoodle could expand into this market. One way to do that would be piloting an offering that is directly for families and after-school care providers. They would be able to use their existing library of videos and content, but maybe price it for a consistent income stream. Finally, GoNoodle can maximize the effects of their newest platform SuperNoodle by having it be backed by a university research center or advisory board. This would increase the legitimacy of the platform as a Social-Emotional Learning tool and improve their outputs.

    Threats

    The threats that are posed against GoNoodle come from differing areas. One threat that they face is the increased scrutiny of Social-Emotional Learning as an industry. At the state level, many states are already proposing bills to ban or limit Social-Emotional Learning in schools. Even though surveyed parents agreed that students should learn Social-Emotional Learning skills in school, the industry continues to be targeted by the current political climate (Abrams, 2023). This is not just a threat to GoNoodle, but the industry as a whole. Another threat to GoNoodle is the growing concern with child screen-time. Parents and educators are becoming increasingly concerned with how much screen-time children are consuming. This could make them more cautious to adopt new EdTech platforms like SuperNoodle. One way to combat this is for GoNoodle to emphasize the fact that SuperNoodle can be used in classroom settings. This eases the concern that children will need to use devices again at home for platforms.
