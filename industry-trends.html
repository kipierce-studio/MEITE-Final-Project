01

Wellbeing-as-a-Service

Turning emotional health into a measurable outcome

The Social-Emotional Learning industry is undergoing a fundamental shift in how student emotional health is managed and measured. Social-Emotional Learning was once a largely unquantifiable classroom practice, it is increasingly being treated as a data-driven outcome. Information is now being tracked, reported, and tied directly to school accountability metrics (SNS Insider). This model, commonly referred to as Wellbeing-as-a-Service, treats emotional health the same way as other student measurable outcomes, with dashboards, benchmarks, and longitudinal tracking (SNS Insider). The market is reflecting this shift more and more clearly. In 2024, the Solutions segment, which included Social-Emotional Learning platforms, assessment tools, and analytical systems, dominated the Social-Emotional Learning platforms. In the analysis, the shift toward analytics and assessments accounted for 73% of total revenue (Social And Emotional Learning Market To Reach $27.73Bn By 2033, n.d.). Companies like Panorama Education led the charge, enhancing their analytics engine with updated behavior-tracking that improved their accuracy by 42% and increasing their teacher engagement with dashboards by more than 55% across participating schools (SEL (Social Emotional Learning) Market Trends 2026–2035, n.d.). Federal investment is also supporting this transition with various grants, totalling $87 million in 2024, focusing on supporting evidence-based, data-supported Social-Emotional Learnings strategies in schools (“Social and Emotional Learning Market,” n.d.). In addition, the House of Representatives approved $260 million to be dedicated to measurable holistic child development outcomes, showing more and more push for Social-Emotional Learning to be used in schools as a measurable wellbeing service (“Social and Emotional Learning Market,” n.d.). This shift may change the way that schools receive federal funding down the road, with weight being put on behavior outcomes and student wellbeing, rather than just academic outcomes.

02

AI-Powered Personalized Social-Emotional Learning

Technology as a SEL Driver

As artificial intelligence grows across the globe, it is also becoming a disruptive force in social-emotional learning as an industry. Today, AI-driven emotional learning systems are being increasingly integrated within social-emotional learning frameworks. Offering interactive simulations, role-playing environments, and real-time feedback, AI has opened the door for new mechanisms to cultivate emotional competencies (ResearchGate 2024). A 2024 systematic review published in the Journal of Research in Innovative Teaching and Learning found that AI technologies, including virtual reality, chatbots, gamification, and wearable devices, present significant opportunities in the social-emotional learning space (Sethi & Jain, 2024). What makes AI particularly powerful in this space is its capacity for real-time personalization. AI tools give users the ability to track emotional patterns over time, flag concerns to educators, and adapt support based on individual student needs (Can Emotional Intelligence Be Taught by AI?, n.d.). This type of usability gives a level of differentiation that has never been seen before in the social-emotional learning space, especially for teachers that are overwhelmed with a full classroom. However, the integration of AI into Social-Emotional Learning is not without ethical concerns. Researchers caution that without intentional design that promotes high-fidelity human interaction, students may experience what scholars call “social deskilling” (Sethi & Jain, 2024). Social deskilling is the erosion of their capacity for empathy, negotiation, and non-verbal attunement (Sethi & Jain, 2024). In order to implement AI effectively, researchers believe it will need to be used as a tool to amplify human connection rather than substitute it (“AI Tutoring in Schools,” n.d.).

03

Physical Activity as a Core Social-Emotional Learning Strategy

SEL as a Movement Tool

Among some of the most significant scientific advancements in Social-Emotional Learning is the evidence that physical movement is not just compatible with social-emotional learning, but it is a driver of it. In a 2024 landmark review and meta-analysis published in the Review of Education by Wiley examined 32 separate intervention studies and found a consistent positive effect of physical activity interventions in elementary school children’s social-emotional learning outcomes (Moon et al.). This was a benchmark moment for social-emotional learning, as it was the first meta-analysis of its kind. This data moved physical activity from a wellness strategy to a data-driven social-emotional learning tool (Moon et. al). More studies have continued to build evidence for physical education implementation in Social-Emotional Learning. In 2025, Frontiers in Psychology carried out a study involving 260 students grades four through six. In this study, they found significant improvements in teamwork, self-awareness, and creative thinking amongst students who received Social-Emotional Learning through physical education classes, compared to students who received standard physical education instruction (Sindinani, et al.). A 2024 review in the Journal of School Health called explicitly for programs designed to advance Social-Emotional Learning and physical activity simultaneously, noting a historical trend for educators to prioritize physical fitness, while sidelining Social-Emotional Learning (Jackson, et al.). Platforms like GoNoodle, which are built at this intersection, are now being validated by scientific evidence and growing in the field.
