A Crisis That Demanded
an Industry

Two decades of rising youth mental health struggles, a smartphone revolution, and the educators who refused to look away — this is how Social-Emotional Learning became a global imperative.

As the global education sector grapples with the rising mental health crisis in young students, Social-Emotional Learning has evolved from supplemental education into a multi-billion dollar technology-driven industry.

This ePublication provides an in-depth analysis of the Social-Emotional Learning industry, exploring the transition from traditional classroom instruction to a dynamic ecosystem where mental health, physical movement, and data analysis intersect.

Youth Mental Health: A Nation Reckoning with Its Children

For more than two decades, a growing number of US youth have been presenting to emergency rooms for mental health issues. The numbers tell a stark story — but behind every statistic is a student, a classroom, and a community struggling to respond.

Suicide rates among young people increased 85.3% between 2007 and 2017. More than 1 in 5 high school students reported seriously considering suicide in the previous year. 9.5% attempted suicide, and 2.3% made attempts requiring medical care.

The Smartphone Generation

With smartphones landing in the hands of younger generations, educators saw dramatic changes in how students communicate, treat others, how isolated they feel, and how they perceive themselves.

📱

Always Connected

Constant connectivity blurred the line between school and social life, making emotional regulation harder for developing brains.

🔇

Rising Isolation

Despite being more "connected" than any generation before, rates of loneliness and social disconnection climbed steadily.

🔄

The SEL Response

Rather than removing technology, educators worked toward helping students use social media in ways that support mental health and wellbeing.
