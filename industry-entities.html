Industry Analysis

Identifying the Major Players

The Social-Emotional Learning industry spans hundreds of platforms, nonprofits, and curriculum providers — but three entities stand apart, each representing a distinct and dominant approach to defining, researching, and delivering SEL at scale.

Why These Three?

Each entity below was selected because it leads its category on a distinct, evidence-based dimension of the SEL industry — not simply because it is well known. Together they represent three different but complementary roles: the framework-setting authority, the research-driven emotional intelligence model, and the evidence-based curriculum provider.

01
CASEL
Collaborative for Academic, Social, and Emotional Learning
Framework Authority

CASEL holds a different kind of power when it comes to the Social-Emotional Learning space as an industry. The Collaborative for Academic, Social, and Emotional Learning is widely regarded as the nation’s leader in supporting and influencing Social-Emotional Learning practices. They are a structural player in this field, rather than a commercial player (“What Is SEL?,” n.d.). Their five-competency framework is one of the most ubiquitous models for organizing Social-Emotional Learning (Niemi, 2020). CASEL functions as less of a competitor in the market and more of the market’s rulebook. They frame implementation as systematic, believing it is a long-term process that goes beyond just teaching a set of skills (“What Is SEL?,” n.d.). They aim to embed Social-Emotional Learning into every aspect of daily school life and believe it is a practice that should be used by schools, families, communities, and beyond (“What Is SEL?,” n.d.). CASEL was chosen as a major player because they hold leverage at such a scale that money cannot buy; for someone to compete with the CASEL they would need to replace their approach at a conceptual level. Having a dual role as standard-setter and evaluator of other’s approaches, CASEL separates itself from every curriculum provider and assessment company (“What Is SEL?,” n.d.).

Evidence of Major-Player Status
  • Originated the CASEL 5 framework, now the universal language of SEL used industry-wide since 1994
  • Authored the first formal SEL guidelines via Elias, Zins, and Weissberg's 1997 book establishing 39 implementation guidelines
  • Cited as the foundational source across virtually every competing curriculum and program's design and marketing
  • Continues to publish implementation tools that shape federal and state policy guidance on SEL

02
RULER
Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence
Research & Emotional Intelligence Authority

RULER’s standing in the field rests less on commercial reach and more on research. RULER is an evidence-based framework for cultivating emotional intelligence and is now implemented in more than 5,000 schools worldwide (“What Is RULER?,” n.d.). They aim to improve the lives of millions of children and adults. RULER is not just a curriculum seller, like Second Step. It started inside a university research center and is now adopted across large districts like New York City (“What Is RULER?,” n.d.). RULER puts out skill development tools that begin with staff and professional learning. Staff then bring their learning into classroom instruction and uplift students. Finally, RULER has content available to families for continued engagement and education. Being housed at Yale gives RULER a layer of legitimacy that is hard for commercial enterprises to compete with. RULER is designed to improve student connectedness. Schools that have implemented RULER, compared to those without, have demonstrated less bullying and aggressive behaviors after one year, as well as higher student connectedness with peers and teachers (“Evidence for RULER,” n.d.). RULER’s growth is tied to the ongoing peer-reviewed research at Yale, and does not need to rely on sales teams to funnel their products as much. RULER is currently helping lead Connecticut’s first statewide emotionally intelligent education system. This level of statewide adoption further shows their institutional trust across the nation. Students who receive RULER perform better academically and are rated by their teachers as having better work habits (“Evidence for RULER,” n.d.). RULER was selected as a major player because they reach schools and children worldwide. They have established themselves as a research-backed program in the Social-Emotional Learning field. The Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence has allowed them to carry a level of academic rigor and peer-reviewed validation that CASEL and Second Step lack. Their tool has become a widely accepted model for schools and districts that are seeking a research-first approach.

Evidence of Major-Player Status
  • Developed and maintained by the Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence, providing direct academic and research credibility
  • Built on the foundational emotional intelligence theory established by Salovey and Mayer in 1989, giving it a direct lineage to the scientific origins of the field
  • Implemented in thousands of schools nationally and internationally as a leading research-based emotional intelligence curriculum
  • Continuously studied and refined through ongoing university-led research, distinguishing it from purely commercial competitors

03
Committee for Children — Second Step
Evidence-Based SEL Curriculum Provider
Curriculum & Classroom Implementation Leader

The Committee for Children has been a leader in advancing the safety and well-being of children since 1979. Through research-based programs, like Second Step, they aim to create safer, more supportive environments. Second Step was developed in 1986 as a school curriculum for Social-Emotional Learning. They were first adopted by the Los Angeles Unified School District, and are now adopted in more than 46,000 schools across all 50 states, reaching roughly 26.9 million children nationwide (Second Step® Evidence-Based Human Skills K-12 Programs, n.d.). They now operate as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, despite their functioning as a market leader. The program operates as an evidence-based curriculum program that has a depth of research behind itself. Second Step’s classroom based social-skills has a distinct curriculum for all grades K-12. Their programming has been shown to reduce impulsive, high-risk, and other aggressive behaviors in children (WWC | Second Step, n.d.). They have also seen an increase in children’s social competence and other protective factors after just one year of Second Step Curriculum (WWC | Second Step, n.d.). In addition to research backing, Second Step has received federal validation through funding and recognition as a Model Program by the US Department of Education (Second Step® Evidence-Based Human Skills K-12 Programs, n.d.). Second Step was chosen as a major player in Social-Emotional Learning because of their reach across the United States and prevalence in schools. They are one of the longest running programs, and one of the most widely implemented. With CASEL setting the framework and RULER providing research driven models, Second Step covers the implementation of tools for Social-Emotional Learning.

Evidence of Major-Player Status
  • One of the most established and widely adopted evidence-based SEL curricula, with over two decades of classroom implementation
  • Backed by Committee for Children, a long-standing nonprofit dedicated specifically to child safety and SEL curriculum development
  • Independently studied for its impact on student social competence and behavior outcomes across diverse school settings
  • Provides a structured Pre-K through 12th grade scope-and-sequence model that has become a benchmark for curriculum-based SEL delivery

How the Three Players Fit Together

These three entities are not competitors so much as complementary forces occupying distinct layers of the same industry. CASEL sets the definitional framework that the entire field operates within. RULER, through the Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence, sits at the research and theory layer, providing an academically rigorous model of emotional intelligence grounded in continuously updated science. Committee for Children's Second Step sits at the implementation layer, translating shared SEL principles into a structured, teachable curriculum that classrooms can adopt directly.

Understanding SEL as an industry requires understanding all three layers simultaneously: a framework without research risks becoming outdated, research without classroom-ready implementation remains theoretical, and curriculum without a shared framework has no common standard to build toward. The major players profiled here are major precisely because each is indispensable to a different layer of that structure.
