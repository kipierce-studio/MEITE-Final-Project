The CASEL 5 Framework

After their founding conference in 1994 in Kalamazoo, Michigan, the Collaborative for Academic, Social, and Emotional Learning established the CASEL 5. The CASEL Five addresses the five broad, interrelated areas that the Collaborative for Academic, Social, and Emotional Learning deems necessary competency areas for children to master. These competencies can be taught and applied at a variety of developmental stages, and are important to know and be able to do. The Collaborative for Academic, Social, and Emotional Learning believes that these five areas are crucial for academic success, school and civic engagement, health and wellness, and fulfilling careers (Casel's SEL framework: n.d.).

Self-Awareness hover or click

Self-Awareness

The CASEL defines this as the abilities to understand one’s own emotions, thoughts, and values and how they influence behavior across contexts (Casel's SEL framework: n.d.)

Self-Management hover or click

Self-Management

The CASEL believes it is important for one to be able to manage their thoughts, emotions, and behaviors effectively; this should be able to be done across situations and to achieve one’s own goals and aspirations (Casel's SEL framework: n.d.)

Social Awareness hover or click

Social Awareness

For the CASEL, being socially aware is not just the ability to understand one’s own perspective, but it is also being able to understand other’s perspectives and empathize with them, no matter the background or culture of the other person. This means they are able to feel compassion for others, understand broader norms for behaviors, and recognize their communities as supports (Casel's SEL framework: n.d.)

Relationship Skills hover or click

Relationship Skills

Being able to establish and maintain healthy and supportive relationships with diverse groups of people is a skill that the CASEL prioritizes. They believe that individuals should be able to communicate, listen effectively, work collaboratively, and navigate settings with differing cultural and social demands (Casel's SEL framework: n.d.)

Responsible Decision-Making hover or click

Responsible Decision-Making

According to the CASEL, the ability to make caring and constructive choices about one’s own personal behavior and social interactions across diverse situations is the epitome of responsible decision-making. This also includes evaluating the benefits and consequences of actions (Casel's SEL framework: n.d.)
