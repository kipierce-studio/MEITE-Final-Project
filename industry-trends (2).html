Three External Forces Impacting SEL

Social-Emotional Learning does not exist in a vacuum. Three major external forces — political, financial, and public health — are actively shaping how the industry grows, contracts, and responds.

01
External Threat

The Critical Race Theory Debate — SEL as a Political Target

Political Headwind

In recent years, Social-Emotional Learning has become entangled in one of the most contentious culture war debates in American education. Critics — primarily, but not exclusively, from conservative political movements — have argued that SEL is being used as a vehicle to introduce Critical Race Theory (CRT) into K-12 classrooms. This framing positions SEL not as a mental health and skill-building tool, but as an ideologically driven curriculum designed to shape students' views on race, identity, and social justice.

The conflation of SEL with CRT has had real, measurable consequences for the industry. Across more than 19 states, legislators have introduced or passed bills restricting certain aspects of SEL instruction, with some explicitly naming social-emotional learning programs as targets. In several districts, school boards have faced intense public pressure to remove or rename SEL programs entirely, even when those programs had demonstrated academic and behavioral benefits. Parents who had never engaged with SEL curricula suddenly found themselves at the center of school board meetings debating what emotional learning content their children should or should not receive.

The attacks have put SEL providers in a difficult position. Companies and nonprofits that spent decades building evidence-based frameworks now face a political environment where the research base is secondary to public perception. CASEL, as the field's defining authority, has explicitly responded to these critiques by emphasizing that the CASEL 5 competencies are academically grounded and content-neutral. However, the political momentum behind these attacks has proven difficult to reverse, and the industry continues to navigate a landscape where the same programs that educators overwhelmingly support are simultaneously being legislated against in the same states.

Key Evidence
  • 19+ states have introduced or passed legislation restricting or scrutinizing SEL programs as of 2024
  • EdWeek Research Center (2026) found that more than 3 out of 3 educators support SEL instruction — demonstrating the disconnect between educator demand and political opposition
  • CASEL publicly responded to CRT conflation, emphasizing that the CASEL 5 framework is academically grounded and not ideologically driven
  • Multiple school districts have removed or rebranded SEL programs in direct response to political pressure, even where outcome data supported their continuation
⚠️ Industry Implication: This trend creates a two-tiered market where SEL adoption is increasingly determined by political geography rather than evidence. Companies operating nationally must navigate drastically different regulatory environments state by state, and content that is welcome in one district may be banned in the next.
02
External Force

Department of Education Funding — A Shifting Financial Landscape

Policy & Funding Pressure

Federal education funding has long been one of the most powerful external forces shaping what schools can and cannot prioritize. For Social-Emotional Learning, this relationship is direct: when federal funding flows toward holistic student outcomes, SEL programs expand. When it contracts or shifts priorities, schools are forced to make cuts — and non-academic programs like SEL are often the first to go.

The landscape shifted significantly with the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021, which injected $123 billion into K-12 education with explicit encouragement for investment in student mental health and social-emotional wellbeing. This created an unprecedented funding window for SEL programs nationwide, driving the market growth that brought the industry to $4.0 billion by 2024. However, that window was time-limited, and as ARP Act funds expire, districts that built SEL infrastructure on emergency pandemic funding now face difficult sustainability questions.

Compounding this, the Department of Education under recent federal leadership has proposed significant budget restructuring. Proposed cuts in 2025 have raised concerns across the education sector about the long-term availability of Title I and Title IV-A funding streams, both of which the Every Student Succeeds Act opened as pathways for SEL investment. For smaller districts that relied on these streams to fund SEL coordinators, curriculum licenses, and professional development, the proposed cuts represent a direct threat to the programs they built.

Key Evidence
  • The American Rescue Plan Act (2021) injected $123 billion into K-12 education, fueling the SEL market surge to $4.0B by 2024
  • ESSA (2015) created Title I and Title IV-A funding pathways specifically applicable to SEL programs — now under review
  • $87 million in federal grants in 2024 specifically targeted evidence-based SEL strategies, signaling continued but conditional federal support
  • As ARP Act funds sunset, districts are being forced to evaluate which pandemic-era investments are sustainable without emergency funding
📊 Industry Implication: Federal funding is both the industry's greatest opportunity and its greatest vulnerability. The companies best positioned to weather funding shifts are those with free or low-cost core offerings — like GoNoodle — that do not depend on district budget cycles to maintain adoption.
03
External Driver

The Youth Mental Health Crisis — The Demand That Won't Go Away

Public Health Urgency

Beneath the political debates and funding cycles, a persistent and worsening public health crisis continues to drive demand for Social-Emotional Learning at a level that no legislation can fully suppress. Youth mental health in the United States has been deteriorating for more than two decades, and the data is striking in its consistency. Suicide rates among young people increased 85.3% between 2007 and 2017. More than 1 in 5 high school students reported seriously considering suicide in the previous year, with 9.5% making an attempt and 2.3% requiring medical care as a result.

The COVID-19 pandemic dramatically accelerated these trends. The 2021 National Survey on Drug Use and Health reported that 4.9 million adolescents aged 12-17 had experienced a major depressive episode in the prior year, a number that has continued to rise. School counselor-to-student ratios remain far below the levels recommended by the American School Counselor Association, meaning that most students who need mental health support in school have no adequate access to it.

This crisis functions as the underlying demand engine of the entire SEL industry. No matter how contentious the political environment becomes or how tight federal budgets grow, the reality of students presenting with anxiety, depression, trauma, and social disconnection in classrooms across the country creates a persistent need that educators cannot ignore. For SEL companies, this means the core market need is durable — even if the political and financial conditions for meeting that need are not.

Key Evidence
  • Youth suicide rates rose 85.3% between 2007 and 2017, representing the foundational public health crisis driving SEL demand
  • More than 1 in 5 high school students seriously considered suicide in a given year; 9.5% made an attempt
  • 4.9 million adolescents aged 12–17 experienced a major depressive episode in 2021, per the National Survey on Drug Use and Health
  • School counselor-to-student ratios remain below recommended levels nationally, leaving SEL tools as the primary scalable response
🌟 Industry Implication: The youth mental health crisis is the most durable demand driver in the SEL industry. It ensures that even as political headwinds and funding fluctuations create market volatility, the underlying need for SEL tools will not disappear — making this an industry with long-term structural demand regardless of short-term conditions.

How These Three Forces Interact

These three external forces do not operate independently — they are actively in tension with each other, creating a complex environment for the SEL industry to navigate. The youth mental health crisis creates undeniable demand; federal funding has historically created the financial infrastructure to meet that demand; and political resistance around CRT has created a counterforce that threatens both the funding and the cultural permission for schools to act on it.

The result is an industry that is simultaneously growing and under threat. Companies that understand this three-way tension — and can position themselves as evidence-based, politically neutral, and financially accessible — are best positioned to thrive regardless of how the political and funding landscape shifts. GoNoodle's free, movement-based model, grounded in peer-reviewed research, reflects exactly this kind of positioning.
